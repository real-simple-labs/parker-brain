# Process — Atypical Text Placement

Put the copy somewhere unexpected but relevant — written on skin, in the condensation on a bathroom mirror, in sand, on a sidewalk, in the dust on a car. The novelty of *where* the message appears is the concept, and it is a named format tag (`Atypical Text`), not a one-off trick.

## When to pick

- **Best for:** brands with a physical context the audience would recognize instantly — a bathroom for hair care, a body part for a pain product, a kitchen surface for food. Strong on cold traffic because the novelty stops the scroll before the copy is even read.
- **Worst for:** categories with no obvious surface, and premium brands whose visual language cannot absorb a hand-lettered look without cheapening.

## Relevance is the whole rule

The surface has to make sense for the brand, so the viewer immediately registers *why* the message is there. A message written in bathroom-mirror condensation reads instantly for an anti-dandruff brand — of course it is there, that is where you notice the problem. The same message in sand would be nonsense.

Writing on the body is unreasonably effective. A headline written across a foot for a nerve-pain product, across a stomach for a bloating product — it stops people who have that problem in that place. The specificity of the body part does the targeting.

## Execution

1. **Start from the problem's location.** Where does the customer experience this? That physical place is the first candidate surface.
2. **Then consider the moment.** Where are they when they notice it? The bathroom mirror, the changing room, the car at 3pm. Moments are as recognizable as places.
3. **Test the "of course" reaction.** A stranger seeing the surface should immediately understand why the message is there. If the surface needs explaining, it is decoration.
4. **Keep the message short.** These surfaces cannot carry long copy — condensation, skin, and sand all limit legibility. This is a headline-led format and the word cap applies.
5. **Let the surface carry the novelty; the copy still has to pass the stranger test.** A clever placement with a headline that assumes brand knowledge is a wasted stop.
6. **Specify the render precisely.** How the text sits on the surface — smeared, indented, wet, dusty — is what makes it read as real rather than pasted on. Hand to `ai-static-ad-generation.md` with that detail.

## Output content

- The surface and why it is relevant to this category, in one line.
- The copy as it appears on the surface.
- The physical treatment — how the text is formed, how it interacts with the material.
- The rest of the frame: what surrounds it, where the product sits.

## What never to do

- Pick a surface for novelty alone. Irrelevant surfaces read as an agency being clever.
- Write more copy than the surface could physically hold.
- Let the placement excuse a headline that fails the stranger test.
- Leave the physical treatment unspecified — text pasted flat on a surface breaks the whole effect.
