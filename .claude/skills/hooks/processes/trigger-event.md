# Process — Trigger-Event Hooks

Open on the single concrete moment the problem stopped being private and became undeniable — almost always because another person registered it.

The format and its psychology live in `parker-system/creative-strategy-context/hooks.md` (#22); that doc is the source of truth if the two ever drift. This is the execution playbook — how to write it well, not what it is.

## Psychological framework

- **Self-perception through another's eyes** — the problem becomes real when someone else sees it, which is why the witness in the scene does more work than the condition.
- **Recognition over persuasion** — the viewer is not being told they have a problem, they are being shown a moment they have already had.
- **Anticipated shame** — for viewers who have not had the moment yet, it works as a near-miss they want to prevent.
- **Specificity as proof** — an oddly specific moment reads as lived rather than written; generic versions of the same beat do not land.
- **Compressed stakes** — one scene carries the emotional weight a minute of problem-explanation would take.

## Required inputs

- A real trigger moment sourced from customers — reviews, surveys, comments, community threads. Mine for it per `customer-review-mining-method.md`, where it is a named qualifying signal. Never invented.
- The verbatim line the customer said or thought in that second.
- The witness — who else was in the moment, and what they did or said.
- Brand voice, ICP, and the brand's claims constraints, since these moments frequently sit in health, body, or appearance territory.

## When to pick

- **Best for:** problem-aware audiences who have been tolerating the problem and have not acted. Categories where the real cost is social and relational rather than functional — appearance, odor, hair, skin, weight, hearing, teeth, home condition, pet health. Products whose honest benefit is dignity rather than performance. Animation, motion, and song formats, where a rendered scene can stage a moment a creator cannot credibly perform. Longer problem-solution builds where the moment sets up an extended middle.
- **Worst for:** genuinely unaware audiences with no lived moment to recognize. Low-consideration impulse products where the emotional stakes are not real. Brands whose register cannot carry discomfort.
- **Read the zone before defaulting to it on cold traffic.** This is a high-intensity open. `strategy.md` and `hook-psychology.md` #11 carry the caution that opening cold in high intensity usually makes her scroll — the exposure here is doing different work than fear, but the intensity is real and the brand's audience decides whether it survives a cold impression.

## Execution

1. **Source the moment, do not write it.** Pull from the mined trigger events. A fabricated one is the most conspicuous kind of fake in the whole taxonomy, because anyone who has lived the real one can tell instantly.
2. **Stage one moment, not a montage.** A single scene held long enough to land. The power is in the beat, not the sequence.
3. **Put the witness in frame.** Someone else has to register it. A person alone noticing their own problem is a different, weaker hook.
4. **Keep the line the customer's.** The overlay or spoken line is the sentence a real person said or thought in that second, in their register — not the brand's summary of it.
5. **Let the viewer arrive at the problem.** Naming the condition in the hook collapses it back into a standard problem-aware opener.
6. **Resolve it.** A trigger event that opens hard and does not pay off reads as cruelty. The body has to earn the discomfort back.

## Output content

- The exact opening line or overlay, in the customer's register.
- The scene: who is in it, where it happens, and what the witness does.
- The review or source the moment traces to, quoted.
- The held beat before the turn, and where the resolution lands.
- The ICP and the emotion being recognized.
- The claims check, where the moment touches health, body, or appearance.

## What never to do

- Invent the moment. Every other rule here depends on this one.
- Escalate to humiliation by default. The aggressive direct-response end of this mechanic ("my wife left me") converts for some brands and is a brand-level decision, not a default — check `brand-lens.md` and the brand's claims constraints first. Most brands should stage the moment of being *seen*, not abandoned.
- Punch at the viewer instead of the moment. The scene is about a situation, never an accusation. "You're disgusting" is not this format.
- Imply knowledge of the viewer's condition — ad-policy personal-attribute rules apply to health, body, and appearance moments.
- Confuse it with storytelling (#18). That opens a journey and pays off with an arc; this detonates in a single beat and promises nothing, and the recognition is the payoff.
- Confuse it with Trigger *Word* (#14), which is a vocabulary amplifier and unrelated to this format.
